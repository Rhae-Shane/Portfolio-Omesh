KVtek builds power-electronics hardware. I interned on-site in the design loop — board, firmware, and the paper that lets the next person pick it up.

This stay is short and it is real. I left it off the one-page resume. It is here in full.

## PCB work

- Layouts and cleanup in Altium, Eagle, and KiCad
- Placement that a fab can actually assemble — not a schematic that only looks clean
- Reviews with seniors on clearance, pours, and connector footprints before a board went out

## Embedded

- Microcontroller logic in Embedded C and Arduino
- Bring-up: flash, smoke test, then the boring checks (rails, clocks, a pin that is not the pin you thought)
- Firmware that talks to the board we just spun, not a Nucleo that lives in a drawer

## Docs

- Schematics and notes for engineering and R&D
- Enough context that a revision does not start from a WhatsApp photo of the last board

Hardware internships are mostly sitting still and not breaking someone else's reel. I did the layouts, the flash, and the write-up.
