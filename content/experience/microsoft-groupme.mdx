I represented Microsoft GroupMe at AIT Pune. The job was not to ship code. It was to get a messaging product into rooms that already had WhatsApp, Discord, and unofficial Telegram groups.

## What I ran

- Campus promotion: booths, club tie-ups, and class-to-class walkthroughs
- Technical workshops and info sessions — how GroupMe rooms, likes, and calendar events differ from a WhatsApp blast
- Sessions with faculty and student bodies so event comms could live in one GroupMe instead of five forwards
- Office hours for club leads who wanted a room set up without looking lost in front of juniors

## Adoption work

A campus ambassador number that looks good on a resume is worthless if nobody joined. I treated it like a funnel.

- Seeded rooms for incoming batches and existing clubs
- Walked seniors through admin roles so the room survived after I left the session
- Collected the boring questions — "does it work without a Microsoft account on Android?" — and answered them in the room, not in a slide

## What I took from it

Public speaking, product advocacy, and the habit of explaining a feature without a demo that only works on my laptop. Useful later when I had to sell a paywall or a KYC step inside a real app.
